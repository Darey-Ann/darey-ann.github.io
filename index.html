Master's Thesis Project

March 2026

Title: Design Exploration of Camera Monitoring to Foster Trust in Elderly Care Facilities

Abstract:
Japan is rapidly becoming a “super aging society” with the elderly population exceeding 29% in 2025. This demographic shift places significant pressure on elder care facilities due to severe understaffing and an increased risk of unsupervised accidents. Although the Japanese government promotes technological solutions, conventional surveillance systems often face ethical and emotional resistance related to privacy, autonomy, and the feeling of being watched. This research investigates how the design of camera monitoring systems can be redesigned to foster trust and emotional support for residents in care facilities. The study explores whether users can be shifted along an “acceptance spectrum,” from passive tolerance to active preference and trust. Drawing on the Baby Schema (Kindchenschema) and the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), the study examines how the perceived usefulness, user control, and camera form influence acceptance. Using a Research through Design methodology with a mixed-methods, participatory approach, the project progressed through four iterative phases: literature review, user engagement, prototyping, and analysis. Data collection included a perception workshop, a custom “Friendshapes” application to study visual attributes associated with safety and trust, and in-person interviews with residents and staff at elder care facilities by San Ikukai and AS Partners. The design process produced three interaction categories: nurture, reflect, and connect, with two low fidelity prototypes. The first is a plush companion evoking nurturing responses alongside employing its surveillance functions, and the second is an assistant bot, which offers personal health insights to support user agency. Findings show that acceptance depends on a “privacy-for-benefit trade-off,” with greater openness when monitoring provides direct value. Ultimately, this research argues that the discomfort of surveillance is a design challenge rather than solely an inherent technological limitation. By rethinking the physical form and the interactive relationship between the device and the resident, surveillance can be transformed into an ethically aware, supportive service. This study contributes a design framework and a set of principles to guide the development of future monitoring technologies that prioritize human dignity and long-term well-being.

Keywords: Well-being Engineering, Elder care, Camera monitoring, Research through Design, Agetech

Friendshapes: A three.js app

February 2025

This is an application created to support my ongoing research about camera monitoring acceptance in elder care facilities in Japan

With this app, I've asked people to create a shape that feels safe and trustworthy to them. The app was created by utilizing the mathematical superquadric formula and adding additional parameters to create unique sliders.

This exploration is to discover what the trends are for people around me in choosing "friend shapes" and if this shape language can be translated into the design of monitoring cameras in order to make them more trustworthy.
